The practice

What it is

Raja Yoga means the sovereign union — the yoga of the mind rather than the body. It is not a physical discipline, and it has no relationship to postures or breathing exercises.

The practice begins with a single claim, which you are invited to test rather than believe: that you are not the body you are sitting in, nor the stream of thought passing through it, but the point of conscious light that is aware of both. Meditation is the act of standing in that awareness instead of in the noise.

From there, attention turns outward again — towards the Supreme Soul, experienced not as a figure or an image but as a source of light and peace with which a relationship is possible. For some people that relationship is the whole of it. For others, the self-understanding comes first and the rest arrives later. Both are ordinary.

Why the eyes stay open

Most meditation traditions close the eyes. Rajyoga keeps them open — softly, resting on a point of light or on nothing in particular — for a practical reason. A state that only exists behind closed eyelids ends when you open them. Practised this way, the same quality of attention can be held in a meeting, in traffic, at a hospital bedside, in an argument. That is the entire point of it.

What changes

Nothing dramatic, and not quickly. What people report, in roughly this order: sleep improves; the gap between provocation and reaction widens; old resentments start to feel expensive to keep carrying; decisions get easier because there is less noise around them. None of that is guaranteed and none of it is the goal. It is simply what tends to happen when the mind stops being run at full volume.

Try it now

Three minutes, wherever you are

You do not have to wait until you can visit. This is the shape of the practice, in miniature.

  1. 01

    Sit, and let the eyes soften

    Upright but not rigid. Keep the eyes open, the gaze level and unfocused. Do not try to empty the mind — it will not work, and the trying is itself more noise.

  2. 02

    Find the one who is watching

    Thoughts continue. Instead of following them, notice that something is aware of them. Rest your attention there, at a point just behind the forehead.

  3. 03

    Give it a single thought

    Hold one line, slowly: I am a peaceful being. Not as an affirmation to believe, but as a direction to look in.

  4. 04

    Let the light come towards you

    If it helps, imagine a steady source of light ahead of you, and let it reach you rather than reaching for it. Stay a minute longer than is comfortable.

  5. 05

    Stand up slowly, and carry it

    Return to what you were doing without a break in attention. Repeat for one minute, several times a day. That repetition — not the length of any single sitting — is what changes things.
